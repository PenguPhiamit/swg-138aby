## Ranked findings with failing cases

### F1 ? P1, open inherited defect: bot suppression leaves the real-player helper deadlock reachable

Scope: `3dca6772` / `61f5d886` as a release-readiness remedy. Location: `objects/player/events/SpawnHelperDroidTask.h:56,75-76`; scheduling at `PlayerObjectImplementation.cpp:625,3880`; cross-lock behavior in `utils/engine3/MMOEngine/src/system/thread/ReadWriteLock.cpp:253-265`.

The two new guards return for registered server bots. A real player is unregistered and takes the unchanged path: player lock, plain control-device lock, then a droid lock crossed against **the player**, while retaining the device lock. The engine cross-lock temporarily releases/reacquires its supplied player lock under contention; it does not release the device lock.

**Failing schedule:** a character younger than one day, with an existing helper, has two scene-ready helper tasks outstanding. T1 holds player P and device C, encounters contention on droid H, and releases P through the cross-lock. T2 obtains P and blocks on the plain acquisition of C. T1 then blocks reacquiring P while retaining C. This is a P/C cycle even if H subsequently becomes free. `notifySceneReady` has no pending-helper-task deduplication. Being a human player, or being the only human player online, does not exclude two tasks for that same character.

Independent read-only examination of the already-saved `/var/tmp/core3-stacks.txt` and `core3-stacks2.txt` supports this mechanism: in the first dump thread 61 waits at old task line 69 (device lock), thread 57 is at old line 70 in the cross-lock's reacquisition at `ReadWriteLock.cpp:263`, and threads 58-60 wait on that same cross/player lock at old task line 50. The second dump has the corresponding pattern in threads 60/59/57-58/62. These are the saved bot incidents, **not a newly reproduced human incident**, and a stack alone does not supply every ownership edge. The failing schedule above follows the actual source and cross-lock implementation.

**Required:** fix the common helper locking/lifecycle, including duplicate task admission or serialization, and check it with a normal unregistered character as well as bots. Do not simply remove the bot bypass while the simulation is running. This defect predates these commits; the new workaround does not introduce it, but cannot provide live deadlock clearance. No repair or runtime experiment was attempted in this session.

### F2 ? P2, open: the default spawn gate also removes endgame spawns for fully trained solo players

Commit: `b20a46d7`. Locations: `objects/region/SpawnAreaImplementation.cpp:301-304,418`; `managers/player/PlayerManagerImplementation.cpp:4415-4433`; `bin/scripts/mobile/spawn/tatooine/tatooine_hard_krayt_ne.lua:6,15,24,33`.

The test is unconditional across spawn areas and defaults on: `minDifficulty > 10 + 3 * playerLevel`. The ordinary solo-level calculation caps at **25**, so even the strongest solo player's threshold is **85**. The krayt table's four minima are **122, 275, 320, 320**. Every draw from that table is rejected. Group level uses a different scale and may pass those rows; this creates an additional group requirement for world population, rather than only protecting starting towns.

**Failing case, source/data counterexample executed offline:** with the default setting, an ungrouped master enters the northeast krayt area with none of that table's spawns already present. All four rows are rejected; movement/retries cannot create one. Existing creatures or other areas can mask this in a casual visit. Setting the base to zero restores the preceding eligibility rule. This does not claim that every krayt on the whole planet disappears, or that a master can defeat every such creature.

**Required:** scope the protection to approved starter areas, or adopt an explicitly approved eligibility rule that preserves intended high-level wilderness populations. Check novice/solo-master/grouped cases in both starter and endgame areas. The setting is a function-local static: changing configuration after the first call does **not** refresh the cached value; document the restart boundary when testing it.
